# Research — what the best café / drink ads in the US & Europe do (2025–2026)

Method: web search across ad-industry and platform sources (Think with Google / Ipsos ABCD,
Meta creative guidance and safe-zone guides, food-videography and sensory-ads research, Starbucks
craft write-ups). Many publisher pages are blocked by this environment's network policy, so findings
come from the search-result summaries of those sources, not full articles. Treat the numbers as
industry claims, not audited facts.

## 1. What the winning ads have in common
| Principle | Evidence | How film 2 uses it |
|---|---|---|
| **Earn the first 3 s** | Meta: ~47 % of an ad's value is delivered in the first 3 s, 74 % by 10 s. Hook rate > 30 % is the bar. | Frame 1 is already the drink, in macro, mid-motion. No logo intro, no black. |
| **Brand in the first ~5 s** | Google/Ipsos ABCD: show product or logo in the first 5 s. Facebook study of 759 ads: early branding lifts recall (e.g. Absolut logo in 2–3 s → +33 pt recall). | The cup's own printed logo is readable by ~2.5 s. Brand lock-up returns at the end. |
| **Built for sound off** | ~85 % of Facebook video is watched muted. Captions add ~12 % view time. | Every idea is carried by on-screen Arabic type. Sound is a bonus layer, never the message. |
| **Sensory "appetite appeal"** | Food ads with texture-close sound (ASMR: ice, pour, fizz) evoke cravings and imagined taste/smell. | Macro textures (condensation, matcha layer, pearls) + ice clinks, straw, pearl "pops", soft pour. |
| **Slow motion = luxury** | INSEAD research: slow-motion product footage raises perceived luxury. Starbucks/Nespresso craft: slow macro sliders, push-ins, orbits, rim light. | Slow, continuous camera moves on every shot; a light sweep across the products; nothing jerky. |
| **Connect emotionally** | ABCD "Connect": a functional fact delivered as an emotional benefit. | The drink is framed as *your moment* — "خُد لحظتك" — not a price or a list. |
| **One specific CTA** | ABCD "Direct": specific, single-thread CTAs beat generic ones. | One CTA: come try *this* drink today. The Facebook button carries the link/directions. |
| **Short versions convert** | 6–15 s ads usually perform best on Meta. | Besides the 60 s you asked for: a 15 s cut-down and a 6 s bumper from the same build. |

## 2. Format for Facebook (Meta, 2026)
- **4:5 (1080×1350)** is the largest feed format → master.
- **9:16 (1080×1920)** for Reels/Stories: keep text and logo out of the top 14 %, bottom 35 %, sides 6 %.
- H.264, AAC, 30 fps, ≤ 60 s for the hero; burned-in Arabic text (no separate captions needed, nothing is spoken).

## 3. Craft vocabulary we borrow (adapted to stills, all done in code)
- **Macro push-in / pull-back** with a slow ease — the "slider" feel.
- **Anatomy callouts** — thin lines draw to each layer of the drink (premium drink-brand trope).
- **Shape-mask transition** — the brand's own balcony window becomes the portal into the hero shot.
- **Liquid wipe** — a matcha-coloured wave carries us between scenes.
- **Rim light sweep** — a soft specular band passes over the cup and carton.
- **Beat-cut detail montage** — hard cuts on the music's beat between textures, each with its own move.
- **Floating ingredients** — slow-motion boba pearls drifting through frame.
- **Music:** bossa-nova / lo-fi jazz at ~90 BPM — warm Rhodes chords, nylon-style plucks, brushed groove.
  The café-music research puts coffee moods at 60–90 BPM; bossa nova is the classic café sound.

## 4. What we do *not* copy
- Spinning logos, lens flares, shakes, glitch, "speed ramp whoosh" clichés, stock smiling actors.
- Superlatives we cannot prove ("the best in Cairo", "100 % natural", "everyone loves it").
