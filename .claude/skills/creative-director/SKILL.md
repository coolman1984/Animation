# creative-director

Operating policy: `studio/WORKFLOW.md`. This skill owns direction, not rendering.

1. Reduce the job to viewer → one promise/proof → one action. Keep factual claims tied to product evidence.
2. Study at most three useful references for grammar: framing, light, type hierarchy, shot scale, rhythm, transitions and sound character. Do not copy their surface style.
3. If the direction is not fixed, propose up to three genuinely different directions. Each needs one motif, palette/light logic, two font roles, camera behavior, transition family and sound character.
4. Choose one direction before full motion. Build three style frames: hook, proof/product, payoff/CTA. Check phone size and 100% crops.
5. Add `craft` metadata to every planned shot: scale, camera, focal, transition, depthLayers, primaryMotions and audioCue where relevant.
6. Run the production preflight. Creative warnings from `lib/creative.mjs` are prompts for inspection, not automatic taste judgments. Resolve accidental repetition and >2 competing primary motions.
7. Build one 8–12 s proof with a real transition and sound. Expand only after the visual hierarchy, product truth and pacing work.

Rules:
- Never default to the last film's typography, palette, bokeh, grain, arches, liquid, particles or transition trick.
- Variation must serve the message. Different for the sake of different is not direction.
- A flat source photo cannot become a new camera angle. Use graphic 2.5D treatment or request better assets.
- Keep the product/brand readable and geometrically truthful. No invented reflections, packaging details or claims.
- One dominant action at a time; quiet holds can feel more premium than permanent motion.

When a supplied reference is used, read ARTISTIC_FORENSICS.md and the reviewed ARTISTIC DNA. Transfer evidence-linked principles into an original new-subject direction using reference.mjs direct, rather than reuse surface style. Plan attention through contrast: hook, question, quiet inspection and payoff when appropriate. Use context-specific colour/emotion hypotheses; audience retention is unknown without viewer data.
